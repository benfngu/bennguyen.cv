02 / everyday tools1st · CED Hacks

BinIt: a little less bin confusion

Take a photo. Check the material, the mess, and the local rules. A waste-sorting app that explains where an item belongs and points to the municipal source.

  • Node.js
  • Express
  • Anthropic SDK
The idea & the build +

Why we built it

A greasy paper plate and a clean one can need different disposal advice. We wanted a tool that considers an item’s condition alongside its material.

What we made

I built BinIt with two teammates at CED Hacks 2026, where our team took first place. The demo used San Jose disposal rules and returned the municipal source behind its guidance.

03 / cell therapy1st · BioEHSC

A closer look at CAR-T quality

A proposed dual-layer system for spotting problems in CAR T-cell manufacturing, combining Raman spectroscopy with measurements of cell state and metabolism.

  • System design
  • Raman
  • SCENITH
Read the research concept +

My contribution

I led a five-person team and translated T-cell biology into engineering requirements. I wrote a 23-page architecture guide and compiled a 13-paper citation library.

Where it stands

The first-author manuscript is in preparation. This is a proposed architecture, not an experimentally or clinically validated system.

04 / cooperative AIin development

A chess engine that plays with you

Two boards, shared pieces, and a human teammate. A Bughouse engine exploring how an AI can make good decisions when cooperation is part of the game.

  • Python
  • PyTorch
  • Teamwork
Why two boards? +

The question

In Bughouse, captured pieces go to your partner, who can drop them onto their board. A useful move has to account for both games, incomplete information, and a ticking clock.

The collaboration

I'm building the engine with a classmate under PhD mentorship. We’re exploring an AI that plays one board as a human’s teammate.
